Facts

Did you know

In 2003 the 1930 Shanghai Concert Hall (about 5,650 tonnes) was lifted 3.38 m and slid 66.46 m southeast with 59 hydraulic jacks, away from the noise and exhaust of the Yan'an Elevated Road. It reopened on 1 Oct 2004.

Stand outside by the elevated road and picture the whole theatre being lifted and slid sideways; it still hosts concerts.

Shanghai Concert Hall (Cadillac Shanghai Concert Hall), 523 Yan'an East Road, Huangpu (free to see from outside)Amap (opens Amap, or its website, in a new tab)Documented
Caveat and sources

Tonnage and jacks are confirmed by a 2003 wire report (The Star) and Yicai (2024). English Wikipedia's version (moved 2007, 5,800 t) is wrong; zh-Wikipedia gives 1 Jan 2005 for the whole project's end. A 2026 season programme lists Steven Osborne on 29 Nov (single-source); confirm on the hall's ticketing.

Did you know

The Waibaidu Bridge (Garden Bridge), opened 20 Jan 1908, is usually billed as China's first all-steel riveted bridge. In 2008-09 it was floated off on barges for about ten months of repairs, then floated back.

Look for the rivet heads on the steel truss: about 63,000, roughly 40% of the total, were replaced with look-alike copies in the 2008-09 repair.

The Bund at dusk 外滩（中山东一路） Documented
Caveat and sources

'First' is a local boast: a steel railway bridge over the Yellow River at Zhengzhou opened in Nov 1905. Dates vary by source (1907 or 1908). About 63,000 of roughly 160,000 rivets were replaced (Hongkou district account). It is a working road; a Sep 2026 notice closed it to vehicles only for a parade.

Did you know

In 1993 the Bund Signal Tower, a reinforced-concrete tower of about 45 to 50 m built in 1907, was moved 22.4 m toward the river in one piece, only a few centimetres a day, for road widening.

A tower that size was inched along without being taken down, and since October 2026 you can go inside: history display below, coffee room above.

The Bund (old International Settlement waterfront) and the reopened Bund Signal Tower 外滩 与 外滩信号台 Documented
Caveat and sources

Reopening reported by Guangming Daily (18 Oct 2026), which gives no hours or price. 10:00-22:00 comes from two ifeng self-media posts of 19 Oct that relay a bureau list: check on arrival. Ticket price and booking rules were not found, probably free: check at the door. Height is 45 m (China Daily) or nearly 50 m (2026 reports).

Did you know

The Chinese Communist Party's official birthday, 1 July, is not the day its First Congress opened: that was 23 July 1921, in a Shanghai lane house. Police searched it on 30 July; the delegates moved to a boat in Jiaxing.

The date 1 July was fixed later (Central Committee, June 1941); the meeting opened in a house in Xintiandi.

Memorial Hall and Site of the First National Congress of the CPC (with the Bowen Girls' School dormitory) 中共一大纪念馆 · 中共一大会址（含中共一大代表宿舍旧址／博文女校旧址） Documented
Caveat and sources

The 23 July date was fixed by later research. The site is free and, per a 28 Jul 2026 bureau notice, needs no booking; open 9:00-17:00, last entry 16:30, closed Mondays (including 30 Nov). The delegates' dormitory at 太仓路127号 is booking-only by WeChat; a foreign visitor may not be able to book.

Did you know

Xintiandi's shikumen lane houses grew out of refugee booms: after the 1853 Small Swords uprising, more than 20,000 Chinese lived in the foreign concessions by 1854-55. Brick lane houses with stone-framed doors replaced timber huts.

Walk the lanes and look at the stone-framed doors: by the 1930s this house type sheltered roughly half to two-thirds of Shanghai's residents.

Xintiandi shikumen lanes (North Block off Taicang Road, South Block around Madang Road), Huangpu, a few minutes from Xintiandi metro stationAmap (opens Amap, or its website, in a new tab)Documented
Caveat and sources

Name: usually explained as 石箍门 (stone-hooped door), since 箍 and 库 sound alike locally; historians give two accounts. Xintiandi was extensively reconstructed. The Shikumen Open House Museum (石库门屋里厢博物馆, Lane 181 Taicang Road) is closed; the building has been a Le Labo shop and cafe since 2023. Ctrip (Nov 2026) lists a free art venue nearby, 新天地喇格纳 (Xintiandi Lagena, 吉安路19号, 19 Ji'an Road); hours not confirmed.

Did you know

Roughly 18,000 to 20,000 Jewish refugees reached Shanghai between the late 1930s and December 1941; no entry visa was needed. In February 1943 the stateless among them were ordered into a Hongkou zone of under 3 sq km.

The museum's Wall of Names, still growing, held 13,732 names in 2014 and 19,672 after October 2025 (museum's own figures).

Shanghai Jewish Refugees Museum (Ohel Moshe Synagogue) 上海犹太难民纪念馆（摩西会堂旧址） Documented
Caveat and sources

Counts differ: about 20,000 (museum) or 18,000 (Encyclopaedia Judaica). 'No entry visa' is from Encyclopaedia Judaica and Wikipedia; the museum stresses consul Ho Feng-Shan's visas, which let refugees leave Europe. Tue-Sun 9:00-17:00, last entry 16:00, closed Monday; adult CNY 20, real-name ticket (bring your passport); free tours in Chinese only; phone 021-55133186 before going, as one-off closures happen.

Did you know

Shanghai's old central street grid is a map of China: the British consul proposed in 1862 that north-south streets take province names and east-west streets city names. In 1865 the Municipal Council adopted Chinese place names for 27 roads.

Look at the street signs as you walk west from the Bund: Jiangxi, Sichuan and Guangxi Roads are provinces, Nanjing and Beijing Roads are cities.

Nanjing East Road and the street grid west of the Bund, old International Settlement (public streets, free, always open)Amap (opens Amap, or its website, in a new tab)Documented
Caveat and sources

Exceptions exist: Wenchang Road runs north-south under a city name (one scholar, Howlett 2022). Hefei Road, near Xintiandi, is a city name, but its naming date was not traced; Xintiandi lay in the French Concession, which named streets differently.

Did you know

Yan'an East Road follows a filled-in creek, the Yangjingbang, once the border between the British Settlement and the French Concession. Filling began in 1914; the road was named Avenue Edward VII in 1916.

The creek's name became the term for pidgin English (洋泾浜英语); rickshaws could not cross the border, and voltage differed (220 V British, 110 V French).

Yan'an East Road (the filled-in Yangjingbang creek), from the Bund west to Xizang Middle Road, about 2 km (public street, free, always open)Amap (opens Amap, or its website, in a new tab)Documented
Caveat and sources

Rickshaw and voltage details come from one article (Xue Liyong, Shanghai surveying institute, 2020). Filling ended in 1915 or 1916 (sources differ). No plaque marks the creek; cross streets such as Jiangxi and Sichuan Roads mark old bridge sites.

Did you know

Shanghai Tower, 632 m and 128 floors, twists 120 degrees from bottom to top. By designer Gensler's own figure the twist cuts wind loads by about 24% versus a rectangular tower, saving about US$58 million in materials.

From the Bund, look for the spiral of the outer glass skin and the V-shaped notch (strake) sliding up one side.

Shanghai Tower observatory, 'Top of Shanghai' (118th floor) 上海中心大厦 上海之巅观光厅（118层） Documented
Caveat and sources

24% and US$58 million are Gensler's own figures. Lifts set a record 20.5 m/s (since beaten) only with a technician; normal rides run at 18 m/s. Do not call the 118th-floor deck (546 m) 'the highest in China': Shenzhen's Ping An Free Sky deck is 541 m above ground but 547.6 m by Guinness's measure, so the title depends on how you measure. Adult CNY 180 and 08:30-22:00 are third-party; one 2026 guide says tickets are real-name, so show your passport.

Did you know

In 1992-93 the Bund's flood-wall line moved 6 to 49 m toward the river (one 2019 retrospective), giving a 1.8 km wall with a car park inside. A 3.3 km tunnel for through traffic opened on 28 March 2010.

At the railing facing Pudong you stand on top of a 1990s flood wall and car park; the old riverbank lay inland of you.

The Bund at dusk 外滩（中山东一路） Documented
Caveat and sources

The 6 to 49 m figure is single-source (The Paper, 2019); Chinese Wikipedia says only 'dozens of metres'. The Bund buildings are lit by a timer, 19:00-23:00 in August 2026; no winter schedule was found, so check on site. Of the tunnel you see only the ramps.

Did you know

People's Square and People's Park were the Shanghai Race Club's third racecourse (1862). On 27 August 1951 the Shanghai military control commission took the land back. The 1933-34 clubhouse on Nanjing West Road is now the free Shanghai History Museum.

A Chinese history article says the gentle curve of Nanjing West Road and Wusheng Road is a leftover of the racetrack.

People's Park marriage market (parents' matchmaking corner) 人民公园相亲角（上海人民公园） Documented
Caveat and sources

English Wikipedia's claim that the whole outline still follows the track is not confirmed; only the curve is documented, by one Chinese article. Museum: free; no booking needed for ordinary visits since 1 Jul 2024, but one May 2026 feature says real-name booking, so bring your passport. Tue-Sun 9:00-17:00, last entry 16:00, closed Monday.

Did you know

Shanghai has the world's first commercial high-speed maglev line: about 30 km from Longyang Road station to Pudong Airport in about 8 minutes. Its design speed is 431 km/h, but regular service is held to about 300 km/h.

You can ride it: the train floats on a magnetic field, and passengers report a speed display in the car.

Shanghai Maglev, from Longyang Road station (Pudong; Metro Lines 2, 7, 16, 18) to Pudong Airport Terminals 1 and 2Amap (opens Amap, or its website, in a new tab)Documented
Caveat and sources

Fare CNY 50, or CNY 40 with your own same-day air ticket. Trains run every 15 to 20 minutes, about 06:45-21:40 (third-party timetable), so check it against your own flight. Some 2026 guides claim 431 km/h in peak windows; no official source confirms it, so plan on 300 km/h.

Did you know

By the usual account, Huang Mingxian of Nanxiang refined the meat bun with jellied filling in 1871; in 1900 two Nanxiang craftsmen opened the Yu Garden shop that became Nanxiang Steamed Bun Restaurant. The craft is national intangible heritage (2014).

Count the pleats yourself: the traditional description is 14, though a modern master's bun is reported with 16, so it is not a standard.

Yu Garden and the old city bazaar 豫园（豫园商城、城隍庙） Widely reported
Caveat and sources

1871 and 1900 are conventional dates, not firm documents (the gazetteer's own source is not cited). The present building is a 1992 reconstruction, upgraded in 2026. Address: the Shanghai government page says 87 Yuyuan Road, Michelin says 85; show the driver 南翔馒头店 and 九曲桥. Hours 7:30-20:30, about CNY 93 a head; often queued, Michelin advises arriving before 10:30.

Did you know

The one-line 'No dogs or Chinese allowed' sign at Huangpu Park is a legend. What is documented: 1885 park rules banned dogs and bicycles and, separately, Chinese except servants; Chinese were admitted from 1 June 1928.

Historians separate what the posted rules said from the catchy one-line sign, which also appears in the 1972 film Fist of Fury.

The Bund at dusk 外滩（中山东一路） Legend
Caveat and sources

Robert Bickers, who checked the Council's archives, found no board with that wording; the story spread from 1914 (a 1903 diary quotes a different wording). Dates differ: exclusion from 1885 or 1890; the 1928 vote 18 or 19 April; admission 1 June (one source: 1 July). Park: free, open all day (undated listing). Bund History Museum: no 2025-26 evidence it is open.

How the places connect

The Xintiandi lanes and the First Congress site are next to each other. The Bund stories work as one walk: Waibaidu Bridge, Huangpu Park, the promenade, the Signal Tower, Nanjing East Road and Yan'an East Road. The Jewish Refugees Museum (Hongkou) and Shanghai Tower (Pudong) each need a metro or taxi ride. Mondays are closing days for the First Congress site, the Jewish Refugees Museum and the History Museum. Less certain: the official sites of the Concert Hall, Maglev, Shanghai Tower and History Museum would not load, so their hours and prices are third-party; times to each site are not routed. Ranking weighs how surprising the fact is, how well it is documented and how easy the place is to visit.

  • Bund Sightseeing Tunnel (外滩观光隧道)No 2025-26 evidence that it operates, price and hours differ between guides, and two sources say it costs more than ten times a metro ticket.
  • Shanghai Urban Planning Exhibition Center and its 1:500 city model (上海城市规划展示馆)The 1:500 city model has gone; the centre itself is now in More historic (free, WeChat booking compulsory, shut Wednesdays).
