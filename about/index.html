How we check

Read in November 2026

Every entry in this guide carries one of three seals and a short note on what was read and what was not. The note is the point: a place can be well loved and still have an opening time nobody confirmed.

Eat and drink

Each of the 105 places to eat and drink was looked up in the Dianping and Amap apps, between 11 and 28 Nov 2026, with one exception: the dinner booked for the group (first on the Hairy crab page), whose Dianping listing was read on 22 Nov 2026 but whose Amap listing and recent reviews were not read. For the ten Dinner near Xintiandi options, both app ratings were read on 27 Nov; recent critical reviews were read for four of them. Their Checked seal uses an Amap crowd rating from at least 100 reviews as the second source; a smaller count gets Likely. For the nine Heritage and Shunde dinners, Meituan, Dianping and Amap ratings were read on 27–28 Nov; newest critical reviews were read for eight, but not Xinshunji at Lee Gardens. No completed food-safety news search was made for this category. Amap review counts were not shown, so all nine carry a Likely seal. Most have a rating and a review count from both apps; three have no rating and 21 lack a rating or a review count from one app, and their pages say so. The seal also depends on a second source that has to agree: Michelin, a named writer, a ranked list, a crowd rating (which can be the Amap rating itself) or, for a Likely seal, two diners’ write-ups. The eat page explains the kinds used for the Chinese restaurants; for the other places, the entry’s note names the list, article or review it rests on.

Places to see, local secrets and errands

Official and other sources were read for each of these entries and the confidence in the information was recorded: high is Checked, medium is Likely, low is Unconfirmed. The date each entry was read is on its page.

What a seal does not say

A seal describes how well the information could be confirmed, not how good a place is. Checked does not mean recommended, and Unconfirmed does not mean a place is closed or best avoided. Hours, prices and rules change, so check on the day.

Directions and distances

Where a route starts “from Xintiandi station” or from South Huangpi Road station, that station is only a reference point: the two are neighbouring central interchanges (Lines 10 and 13; Lines 1 and 14) next to the site of the First Congress. Distances and walking times are rough map estimates, so allow a few hundred metres either way.

Each place has an Open in Amap link. On a phone with the Amap app it opens the app, and otherwise Amap’s website. Most restaurants and bars open Amap’s own entry for the place; the other places open a search for the Chinese name next to the pin, so check that the first result’s address matches the card. Some in-app browsers cannot open the Amap app; use a regular browser if that happens.

Chinese names and addresses

Chinese names sit beside the English ones. Show the driver on any place shows its Chinese address in large type; tap the address to copy it into a taxi or map app.

What the seals mean

Three states

A seal says how well an entry’s evidence stands, not whether the place is good. Checked never means that anyone confirmed hours, prices or availability with the venue: nobody phoned or visited. Everything was read from the pages and apps listed under each entry.

Checked

Eat and drinkBoth app ratings were read, each from at least 20 reviews, and an independent second source agrees: Michelin, a named writer, a ranked list, or a crowd rating of 4.3 or more from at least 100 reviews. Where that crowd rating is the Amap rating itself, the same rating counts twice, as an app rating and as the second source, and the entry’s note says “Second source: Amap”. For bars, clubs and live music there is also a dated sign, within 45 days of the reading, that the venue is open.

See and errandsThe main facts come from official or primary sources, or from several sources that agree, read in November 2026. A minor detail, such as one day’s opening hours, may still differ between sources; the entry says so.

Likely

Eat and drinkOne or two gaps. The gaps are: a second source that is only diners’ write-ups; an app rating from fewer than 20 reviews or shown without a review count, or no Amap rating; and, for a bar, club or live venue, no dated sign in the last 45 days that it is open.

See and errandsOne good source, or sources that disagree on a main fact.

Unconfirmed

Eat and drinkNo Dianping star rating, both app ratings weak (missing, or from fewer than 20 reviews), or three or more of those gaps.

See and errandsLittle or old evidence. Treat it as a lead, not a plan, and have a second choice ready.
