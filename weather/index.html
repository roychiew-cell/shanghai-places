Late-November weather

Read on 18 Nov 2026 for central Shanghai, when these days were 6 to 12 days ahead, so confidence is moderate for the first days and lower after; forecasts sharpen inside a week.

The short answer

Cool, dry and a little mild

Mornings are mostly 8 to 11 °C and afternoons 15 to 20 °C, with less than one rainy day in the seven shown: only Tue 24 Nov is above 15% (22%). Most models show no cold snap, but one ensemble puts nights at 1 to 3 °C on 24 and 25 Nov, so a cold night cannot be ruled out. Sunset is about 16:52.

Rain is the chance of at least 1 mm that day. The low is the lowest temperature of the calendar day, which is often late in the evening. Treat the table as what to expect and how to pack, not as a day plan. Each forecast system is an ensemble: many runs of one weather model with slightly different starting conditions.

What stands out

  • Tue 24 Nov is the likeliest rainy day, but only at 22%, against about 32% normal for the date. The five ensembles split: ECMWF IFS 41%, NOAA GEFS 29%, ECMWF AIFS 14%, Canadian GEM 14% and UK Met Office 11%.
  • Dry from 25 to 29 Nov: 0 to 5% a day in the ensembles, against 23 to 26% normal for those dates. Mon 30 Nov is 13% against 25% normal.
  • Milder than normal from Thu 26 to Sat 28 Nov: highs of 18 to 20 °C against about 15 °C normal. At 8 to 10 days ahead that is a lean, not a promise.
  • Night lows are where the models disagree most: for Thu 26 Nov the ensemble medians run from 6 °C to 13 °C. Over the last two days six of the nine models ran 0.7 to 1.6 °C colder than the thermometers at Pudong and Hongqiao airports.
  • Wind is breezy, not stormy: the strongest wind of a day is typically 14 to 16 km/h, and on any day there is about a 1 in 10 chance of 20 to 25 km/h (Tue 24 and Sat 28 Nov are the windiest).

What to wear

  • Layers: a sweater plus a light down or wool coat, and a scarf for mornings and late evenings.
  • A compact umbrella is a small precaution: no day shown is above 22%.
  • If cold nights worry you, add a warmer coat or a thermal layer. Heating in hotels and malls is usually on, so shed layers indoors.

How this was made

  • Forecasts: Open-Meteo, read on the date at the top. Nine global models (ECMWF IFS and AIFS, US GFS, German ICON, Canadian GEM, Japan JMA, China CMA GRAPES, UK Met Office, French ARPEGE) and five ensembles for central Shanghai.
  • History: ERA5 reanalysis for 2006 to 2025 within two days of each date, and the Pudong (ZSPD) and Hongqiao (ZSSS) airport thermometers over the same years (Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive of airport reports).
  • Sunset: calculated from the sun's position for central Shanghai, about 16:52 on 24 to 30 Nov. It is a calculation, not a forecast.
  • Limits: the airport thermometers are about 13 km (Hongqiao) and 33 km (Pudong) from the city centre, which can run a little warmer at night. Ensemble coverage thins after 27 Nov.
  • Nothing here is an official forecast: China's national forecast is not read by this site.
